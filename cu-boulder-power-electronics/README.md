University of Colorado Boulder — Power Electronics Specialization
Introduction to Power Electronics, Colorado University Boulder

This directory documents my work through CU Boulder’s Power Electronics Specialization - covering steady-state converter analysis, equivalent circuit modeling, losses, and efficiency. Coursework is applied here through independent LTspice simulation, worked derivations, and measured-vs-theoretical validation.

---

## Portfolio Objectives & Methodology

Every laboratory module nested within this directory transitions core academic concepts into practical engineering assets:
* **Steady-State Converter Modeling:** Applying volt-second balance, capacitor charge balance, and small-ripple approximations to derive exact ideal and non-ideal transfer functions.
* **Simulation Verification:** Utilizing **LTspice** to build, simulate, and stress-test converter topologies, confirming that physical waveforms match theoretical mathematical models.
* **Component-Level Design:** Evaluating power semiconductor switches (MOSFETs, diodes), magnetic core selections (inductors, transformers), and filter capacitors based on ripple requirements and efficiency goals.
* **Control Loop Compensation:** Designing feedback networks, deriving small-signal AC models, and evaluating loop-gain transfer functions to ensure system stability under dynamic load conditions.
